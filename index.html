Iran receives US feedback on a seven-day trust-building plan

Iran has received a US response through Qatari mediators to a proposed seven-day plan intended to reduce tensions and restore normal maritime passage through the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters reports that the current discussions are focused on the sequence of steps rather than agreement on the plan itself. The outcome could influence both regional diplomacy and the security of one of the world's most important energy shipping routes.

Oil climbs as stalled diplomacy keeps energy markets on edge

Oil prices rose on September 30 as markets reacted to stalled US-Iran talks and tighter fuel markets. Reuters reported Brent at $103.50 a barrel at the close of the November contract, while US crude settled at $90.42. The moves show how diplomatic uncertainty can quickly affect energy costs, shipping and inflation expectations far beyond the immediate conflict zone.

Russia extends diesel export restrictions through October

Russia has extended its diesel export ban until the end of October as domestic fuel supplies remain under pressure. Reuters reports that Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil refineries have contributed to supply disruptions, while the wider Iran and Ukraine conflicts are adding stress to global fuel markets. The restrictions could keep pressure on international diesel prices and shipping costs.

France and Spain seek stronger European trade protections

French President Emmanuel Macron and Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez have called for stronger European trade defences, particularly around competition from China. Reuters reports that the leaders also discussed European budget pressures, relations with Britain and the need for greater coordination among EU governments. The debate comes as Europe tries to balance industrial protection with its trading relationships.

Reuters source →

US trade court examines the legality of new global tariffs

The US Court of International Trade is reviewing the legality of new tariffs imposed by the Trump administration on imports from dozens of countries. Reuters reports that challengers argue the administration did not follow required procedures, while the Justice Department says the government carried out sufficient evaluation. A ruling could determine how the tariffs are applied and what legal authority the administration can use.

UN envoy works to contain renewed fighting around the Red Sea

UN envoy Hans Grundberg has met Houthi negotiators in Muscat and officials in Saudi Arabia in an effort to prevent a wider escalation in Yemen. AP reports that renewed fighting and control of strategic coastal areas are creating additional risks for maritime trade through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. Diplomats are trying to separate the Yemen negotiations from the wider regional conflict.

Deadly strikes keep Ukraine war at the centre of European security

Russia and Ukraine exchanged deadly strikes in recent days, with attacks reported in several Ukrainian cities and Russian-controlled areas. AP reports that the violence followed diplomatic discussions at the UN, while concerns about critical infrastructure and the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant remain part of the wider security picture. European governments continue to debate military support and long-term defence planning.

Qatar Airways changes fleet strategy as Iran war disrupts regional aviation

The prolonged Iran war is affecting Gulf aviation, with Qatar Airways retiring its last Airbus A330 passenger aircraft and seconding some pilots to Rwanda's flag carrier, according to Reuters. The airline changes come as jet fuel prices have risen sharply and regional flight operations face continued uncertainty. The effects illustrate how conflict can reach far beyond military and diplomatic institutions.

A turbulent UN week leaves diplomacy with more questions than answers

The latest UN General Assembly produced several important diplomatic contacts but few immediate breakthroughs. Reuters reports that US-Iran discussions, possible limited ceasefire ideas around Ukraine, AI governance and Sudan all featured prominently. The UN is also dealing with financial constraints and a leadership transition as Secretary-General António Guterres prepares to leave office at the end of the year.

AI governance moves from technology debate into international diplomacy

Artificial intelligence is increasingly being discussed alongside traditional security and foreign-policy issues. Reuters reported that governments and technology leaders used the UN week to debate international cooperation, AI security breaches and the rules that should govern rapidly developing systems. The discussion connects technology policy with questions of national security, economic competition and global standards.

Gulf states reassess security arrangements as US military role changes

Reuters reports that Gulf governments are considering alternative security frameworks as the United States reduces parts of its military involvement in the region. Qatar has proposed a regional security plan involving Iran, while Saudi Arabia and the UAE are also pursuing diplomatic channels. The changes could reshape how Gulf states approach defence, regional deterrence and economic security.

Reuters source →

European governments debate how to strengthen collective defence

Security planning across Europe is increasingly shaped by the continuing war in Ukraine and concerns about hybrid threats, critical infrastructure and defence readiness. Governments are discussing procurement, coordination and longer-term support for Ukraine while also responding to pressure on national budgets. The debate is influencing Europe's relationship with NATO and its own emerging defence capabilities.

For international investors and policymakers, geopolitical risk is becoming a more important part of economic planning.
